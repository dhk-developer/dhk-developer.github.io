About me

I studied Chemistry at Imperial College London. The part I liked most was the computing: writing Python to build and check simulation inputs, and then a summer at Cambridge writing code that generated polymer structures and let a force field sort out which ones made sense.

After university I trained in tax for a few months, then joined Xander Talent as a technical consultant, doing data and CRM work for clients. Since October 2024 I've been a Business Analyst at Infotechtion. I interview stakeholders, run research sessions with the people who manage records every day, map how work actually happens and write the requirements that engineering teams build from.

I like the job, and it's taught me a lot about asking the right questions. But the part of my week I look forward to most is building things, and I've been doing more of it every year. Memora started as a small prototype and turned into a game with its own editor, timing engine and story system. Along the way I've made a couple of Windows tools because something annoyed me enough to fix it.

So I'm now looking to move into software development properly. I know I'd be starting as a junior, and I'm fine with that. I think the analysis background helps: I'm used to pinning down what something actually needs to do before building it, and to explaining technical things to people who aren't technical.

How I tend to work

  • I write the rule down as a sentence before I write the code. Most of Memora's hard problems got easier once I did.
  • I measure before I optimise. The editor slowdown turned out to be something I wouldn't have guessed.
  • I try to say clearly what isn't finished, at work and in my own projects.
  • I treat existing data and users as things not to break.

Outside of that

I speak Korean fluently. I draw, and I've sold some of my artwork online. I play a lot of rhythm games, which is where Memora came from.
