About me

I first wrote code properly in my A-level Computer Science class, and at school I ran a daily computer science club for other students. I then went to Imperial College London to study chemistry, which might look like a change of direction, but the parts of the degree I enjoyed most were the computational ones. At one point I wrote a small program to solve stoichiometry equations by reducing them to row echelon form, and my final-year dissertation was about how reproducible it is to build simulation inputs for very large biological molecules. In the summer of 2022 I spent three months at Cambridge writing Python that generated polymer structures and let a force field decide which of them made physical sense.

After graduating I spent a few months training in US and UK tax, which taught me a lot about how much regulated work depends on getting the details right, and then joined Xander Talent as a technical consultant, doing data and CRM work for their clients. Since October 2024 I've been a business analyst at Infotechtion. Most of my week is spent interviewing stakeholders, running research sessions with the people who look after an organisation's records, mapping out how their work gets done in practice and writing the requirements that the engineering team builds from.

I like the job and I've learned a lot from it, particularly about asking the right questions before anything gets built. Even so, the part of my week I look forward to most is the time I spend building things myself, and over the last couple of years that has taken up more and more of my evenings. Memora started as a small prototype and grew into a game with its own editor, timing engine and story system, and along the way I've written a couple of Windows utilities because something on my own PC bothered me enough to fix it.

That's why I'm now looking to move into software development as a job. I know I'd be starting as a junior and I'm comfortable with that. I think the analysis background will help, because I'm used to pinning down what something needs to do before writing it, and to explaining technical decisions to people who don't work with code.

How I like to work

Before writing anything non-trivial I try to describe what it should do in a sentence or two, the same way I'd write an acceptance criterion at work, because most of the harder problems in Memora became much easier once I'd done that. I also try to measure before optimising anything, since the cause of the editor slowdown I describe on the Memora page wasn't what I would have guessed. And I try to be clear about what isn't finished, in my own projects as much as at work.

Outside of work

I speak Korean fluently as well as English. I also draw in my spare time, and I've sold some of my artwork online through a print-on-demand shop.
